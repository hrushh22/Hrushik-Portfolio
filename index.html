ABOUT ME

I'm an MS Big Data Analytics student at San Diego State University, advised by Dr. Ming-Hsiang Tsou, focused on applied machine learning and computer vision research. Back in India, I completed my B.Tech in Artificial Intelligence and Data Science with minors in IoT and Industry 4.0, which mostly meant I had strong opinions about smart sensors before it was cool. Now I'm here, building my career at the intersection of research and engineering on the Pacific Coast.

My research spans RF hardware modeling, drone-captured traffic analysis, zero-knowledge proofs for LLM verification, and multimodal AI, published across ICDAR 2025, IEEE conferences and peer-reviewed journals. I interned at the Indian Institute of Technology Patna's AI-NLP-ML Research Lab and have shipped production ML systems across multiple internships.

I spend most of my time making computers look at videos, fine-tuning models that are already too big, and occasionally winning hackathons by staying up late, most recently Best Proposal at the Snapdragon Multiverse Hackathon 2026, on top of 1st place at the AWS × Cal Poly AI Hackathon and the Most Innovative Award at the Big Data Hackathon San Diego. This past summer I sat with Qualcomm's RFFE hardware team and designed neural networks that predict how real GaAs power amplifiers actually behave, swapping circuit simulators for millisecond inference. I've also taught a 6-DOF robotic arm to pick and place objects in simulation at SDSU's MVP Lab, and built geospatial pipelines for a Caltrans-funded driver-distraction study at DiCE Lab. Outside the lab, you'll find me at the gym, or outside entirely, watching the sun go down.
